The missing link

Cement needs calcium carbonate. The coast throws away 10 million tonnes of it a year. Nobody had connected the two value chains.

01 — SOURCE

Shell waste

We collect discarded oyster, mussel and clam shells from coastal seafood processing plants, restaurant chains and fishing co-operatives. The feedstock is 95–98% calcium carbonate and currently costs the industry money to dispose of.

02 — CONVERT

Calcined to reactive lime

Thermal calcination at 850–900°C drives off CO₂ and converts the shells to calcium oxide. Hydration then turns that into highly reactive lime, the active ingredient of the binder.

CaCO₃  —850–900°C→  CaO + CO₂
03 — BLEND

TerraCreate

Reactive lime blended with natural pozzolan forms calcium silicate hydrate, the binding phase that gives cement its strength. The result is an industry-standard, low-carbon cement.

CaO + SiO₂ + H₂O  →  C–S–H  (the binder)
04 — BUILD

Homes that last

TerraCreate goes to certified builders and NGO housing partners who construct EWS and LIG homes: affordable, durable, and built from a material the coast was throwing away.

The heat advantage

Shells need far less fire than limestone.

Clinker production is the single biggest energy cost in cement. Shell calcination runs several hundred degrees cooler, which is where a large share of the carbon saving comes from.

Lab results

52 mixes. Twenty-one months. 12.8 to 33.9 MPa.

Every TerraCreate formulation has been cast, cured and crushed under IS 4031 protocols. The first batches were friable and full of free lime. The current mix clears the 33 MPa threshold that IS 1489:2015 sets for blended cement.

Independent validation

Three cement firms. Two laboratories.

Between February and March 2024, five external partners tested TerraCreate against their own protocols. All five verified 28-day strength between 33.5 and 34.1 MPa. All five certified it for non-structural applications, and each one flagged something that changed the mix.
